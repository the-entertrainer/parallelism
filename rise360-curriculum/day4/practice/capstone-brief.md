---

## What you are building

A small, finished, published Rise 360 course:

```
Your course title
│
├─ Lesson 1        something the learner needs to know
├─ Lesson 2        something the learner has to decide or do
└─ Quiz            four questions that check the two lessons
```

**Two lessons, one quiz, roughly nine to twelve blocks in total.** That is deliberately
small. A finished small course teaches you more than an unfinished big one, and everything
you learn scaling this up is easier than everything you learn rescuing a sprawl.

**Finished means published.** A course sitting in the editor is not a deliverable.

## Choosing your topic

Pick something **from your own work** that you already know well enough to explain without
research. The capstone is a test of the tool, not of your subject knowledge.

**A good capstone topic:**

- You could explain it to a new colleague in ten minutes.
- It has at least one moment where somebody has to **decide** something — that is your scenario.
- It has at least one thing that is genuinely checkable — that is your quiz.
- Somebody at work would actually be glad it exists.

**Good examples**

| Topic | Lesson 1 | Lesson 2 (decide) |
|---|---|---|
| Handling a chargeback | What a chargeback is and the clock you are on | A customer disputes a charge — what do you do first? |
| Our escalation path | Who owns what, and when it moves | Four incidents; pick the right escalation for each |
| Onboarding a new supplier | The five checks, in order | A supplier is missing one document — proceed or hold? |
| Writing a support reply | Structure, tone, the three things every reply needs | An angry customer, three possible openings |

**Topics that will fight you:** anything you would need to research first; anything with a
compliance sign-off you cannot get by Monday; anything where "what should you do?" honestly
has no wrong answer; anything that needs 40 blocks to be worth doing at all.

If you are stuck at 5 p.m. Friday, take one of the four examples above. Nobody loses marks
for it.

## Requirements

Everything in this list is checked on Monday. It is short on purpose.

### Structure

- [ ] A course (not Microlearning), with a title that reads as a promise to the learner — not `Module 3`
- [ ] A cover photo
- [ ] Exactly two content lessons, plus one quiz lesson
- [ ] Nine to twelve blocks in total across the two lessons

### Blocks

- [ ] At least **four different static block families** from Day 2 — text, statement, list, image, quote, gallery, multimedia, chart or divider
- [ ] At least **one interactive block from Day 3** — accordion, tabs, labeled graphic, process, timeline, flashcards or sorting
- [ ] **One Scenario block**, with at least three scenes, at least one wrong branch, and a **Try again** that returns the learner to the decision rather than scolding them
- [ ] **One Button or Button stack**, with a label that says what happens when it is pressed

### Quiz

- [ ] Four questions minimum, covering **at least three of the four question types**
- [ ] Written feedback on every question — correct and incorrect
- [ ] Passing score set deliberately, and a number that is actually reachable with the number of questions you have
- [ ] Quiz settings reviewed, not left at defaults by accident

### Finish

- [ ] Previewed in **mobile portrait** and fixed whatever that showed you
- [ ] Published to a **Quick Share** link that opens for someone who is not you
- [ ] Exported a **SCORM 1.2** package, tracked by **quiz result**

## A build order that works

Do it in this sequence. Most capstones that go wrong go wrong by starting in the editor.

| | Step | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Outline on paper.** Two lesson titles, one sentence each on what the learner will be able to do. Do not open Rise yet. | 20 min |
| 2 | **Write the scenario first.** It is the hardest thing in the course and the thing most likely to force you to change the lessons. Write the decision, the three responses, the feedback for each, and the ending. | 40 min |
| 3 | **Write the quiz questions.** Now, while the content is still in your head — not at the end when you are tired. | 20 min |
| 4 | **Build Lesson 1.** Static blocks. Fast. | 25 min |
| 5 | **Build Lesson 2** — lead-in paragraph, the scenario you already wrote, the button stack. | 30 min |
| 6 | **Build the quiz** and set the settings. | 20 min |
| 7 | **Preview on mobile.** Fix. Preview again. | 15 min |
| 8 | **Publish and export.** Open your own Quick Share link in a private window to prove it works. | 15 min |

Writing before building is the whole trick. Steps 2 and 3 are two-thirds of the quality and
about a third of the time.

## What "ready" means

This is what gets looked at on Monday. Nothing here is about polish; it is all about whether
the course works for a learner who is not you.

| | Ready | Not ready yet |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | Says what the learner will be able to do | Names a module, a system or a department |
| **Lesson 1** | Under half text; something to look at | A wall of paragraphs |
| **Scenario** | Every option is one a real person might pick | An obviously-correct option and two silly ones |
| **Scenario feedback** | Explains the consequence | Tells the learner they were wrong |
| **Try again** | Returns to the decision | Missing, or dumps them at the end |
| **Buttons** | Label says what happens | "Click here", "Learn more", "Read on" |
| **Quiz** | Feedback on every question, reachable pass mark | Bare right/wrong, 80% pass on four questions |
| **Mobile** | Checked, and something was fixed | Never opened |
| **Link** | Opens for someone else, first time | Never tested outside your own browser |

## Traps we have already seen this week

- **Microlearning instead of Course.** You find out when the quiz option is missing. Check before you build.
- **Scenario written in the editor.** Branching written live turns into a maze you cannot follow. Write it flat, then build it.
- **Passing score of 80% on four questions.** That is 4/4. Do the arithmetic for your own question count.
- **Fill in the Blank with one accepted answer.** Add the spellings, the plural and the obvious synonym, or you will fail people who are right.
- **The lead-in that is missing.** An interaction with no line above it saying what to do gets scrolled straight past.
- **Never previewing mobile.** It is where most of your learners are, and it is where button labels wrap and scenario text crowds.
- **Publishing at 8:55 a.m. Monday.** Publish Saturday. Fix on Sunday. Deadlines are for finished things.

## Where to get help

- **Friday's clinic** — bring the outline, not the finished course. That is when help is cheapest.
- **The team channel** — post the actual screen, not a description of it. Screenshots get answered faster.
- **Articulate's own guides** — the Rise 360 user guide is genuinely good, and it is current. Community answers from three years ago often are not.

and the scenario is the part anybody will remember.
